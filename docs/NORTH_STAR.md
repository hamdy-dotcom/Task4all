# The North Star

Source: *NML Leadership Document, First Edition — "The North Star"*

## The mission (not a metric)

NML builds an intelligent engine that connects the online world to the retail shelf: we forecast what should sell on a shelf, where, when, at what price and for how long. That raises online merchants' sales, improves shelf turnover, and brings goods closer to the buyer.

That is a mission, not a measure. Following four promises at once means following none. One number must carry them all.

## The metric

**Revenue per Shelf Meter**

Total monthly revenue from our shelves divided by total contracted shelf meters. Measured monthly, shown in every leadership meeting.

## Why this number

- **It unites all three sides.** It rises only if the merchant earns, the outlet gains and NML succeeds. No one can lift it at another's expense.
- **It measures the mission, not the activity.** The meter sells more because the forecast was right, and the forecast was right because the engine connected both worlds.
- **It resists cosmetics.** Not liftable by expansion (it is a ratio), nor by stuffing the shelf (dead stock lowers it), nor by signing contracts (every new meter enters the denominator).
- **It makes turnover a goal, not a by-product.** A meter sells only if it turns, and turnover is what convinces merchants to stay and outlets to expand.

## The guardrail

**Share of shelf held by Salla merchants**

The star asks *are we succeeding?* The guardrail asks *are we still us?* Without it, revenue per meter can be lifted with outside suppliers — we would look excellent while drifting away from the reason we were founded. The two are always read together.

## Alternatives we rejected

| Metric | Why not |
|---|---|
| Number of outlets | Lifted by signatures alone; rewards expansion before operations are proven. |
| Total revenue | Hides failing shelves inside the total; grows with size rather than efficiency. |
| Salla merchants on shelf | Close to our purpose, but does not measure whether they actually sold. Kept as the guardrail. |
| Model accuracy | An excellent internal metric that means nothing to a merchant or an outlet. |

## The metric tree

Only four branches lift the star. Every personal metric at NML must map to one of them.

### Forecast Quality
- Recommendation hit rate
- Forecast error: SKU x site
- Decisions from documented rules
- Outlet business-case accuracy

### Execution Discipline
- Planogram compliance
- Stockout rate and detection time
- Shrinkage rate
- Operating cost per outlet

### Supply Fit
- Required category coverage
- Salla merchants on shelf
- First-pass SKU readiness
- Time to first sale · dormancy

### Shelf Quality
- Contracted shelf meters
- Cost per meter vs return
- Outlet renewal rate
- Placement impact on sales

### Rules of the tree
- The star rises only when all four branches rise together.
- Any personal metric that maps to none of the four is deleted or redefined.
- **The branches multiply, they do not add.** Excellent forecasting with poor execution equals a shelf without the right SKU. Excellent execution with unfit supply equals a tidy shelf that does not sell.

## Review rhythm

| Cadence | Who | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Daily — written | Field and operations | Shelf status: what ran out, what sold, what needs intervention. Written, not a meeting. |
| Weekly — 45 min | Leadership | Sold versus forecast · what changes on the shelf next week · blocked decisions. |
| Monthly — 1 hour | Leadership | The star and its four branches · the guardrail · shrinkage and settlement · expand or hold. |
| Quarterly — half day | Leadership | Initiative register review · what we stopped · re-prioritisation · role cards. |

## The one standing rule

The north star is never shown alone. It is always shown with its four branches and the guardrail. A lone number invites the comfortable interpretation; the tree forces the right question: **which branch fell behind, and who owns it?**
